# Azul Sales Playbook

## 01 Start here

This is the working sales guide for one Azul representative who finds prospects, runs discovery, demonstrates the offer, closes the agreement, and coordinates delivery. Focus on South Florida roofing, HVAC, and other home-service businesses. Use English or Spanish according to the buyer's preference.

The first objective is a useful conversation with an owner. The second is an agreed business problem. Recommend a website or an AI workflow only when it fits that problem. Finish every productive conversation with a specific action, an owner, and a date.

### Confirmed starting prices

- **Website Build:** $1,500 one-time build plus $50/month for hosting and ongoing support. The current site describes 8-15 pages, custom design, a content management system, SEO setup, and English and Spanish versions. Confirm the project scope in writing.
- **Custom AI Agent:** starts at $2,000 setup plus $300/month. This is an entry point, not a fixed price for every workflow. Voice, integrations, usage, and more complex work require a scoped quote.
- **Combined purchase:** quote each service and its recurring fees separately. No $3,500 package is authorized in this playbook.

### Find the right page

- Targeting and outreach plan: pages 2-3.
- Cold calls, DMs, and email: pages 4-8.
- Discovery, website demos, and AI demos: pages 9-12.
- Value, pricing, and closing: pages 13-15.

## 02 Choose prospects and the right offer

Spend the first two weeks mainly on roofing and HVAC. Add plumbers, pool companies, landscapers, and other local services once the rep can explain the offer clearly. For an unfamiliar industry, verify the workflow with the founder before promising an agent.

### Research signals

**Website opportunity:** the business has an active Google profile or social presence, but no website, a broken site, an unclear mobile experience, missing service information, or a difficult path to an estimate. Confirm the problem on an actual device. An unattractive design alone does not prove lost business.

**AI opportunity:** the owner describes unanswered inquiries, slow follow-up, repeated questions, scheduling work, or manual copying between tools. Public business hours and a contact form are conversation starters; they do not prove missed leads.

**Combined opportunity:** both problems are confirmed and the team can support delivery. Ask which problem matters first. Phase the work if doing both would make the project harder to approve or launch.

### Prioritize with a simple score

Give one point for each condition: active local business, accessible owner, observed website issue or confirmed workflow problem, ability to take more work, and an agreed reason to act. Use 4-5 points for immediate attention, 2-3 for research or nurture, and 0-1 for low priority. This is a sorting aid, not a prediction of purchase.

### Questions that change the recommendation

**EN:** "Are you trying to get more people to contact you, or handle the people who already do?" "If more inquiries came in next week, could your team handle them?"

**ES:** "¿Busca que más personas lo contacten, o atender mejor las consultas que ya recibe?" "Si la próxima semana llegaran más consultas, ¿su equipo podría atenderlas?"

### Park a deal when

- The owner cannot name a meaningful problem or is at capacity with no growth priority.
- The desired result requires traffic or advertising the website project does not include.
- The AI request has no repeatable task, no access to required tools, or no person for exceptions.
- The buyer needs a custom feature or deadline that delivery has not confirmed.

Record the reason. A sensible next step can be a later conversation or a referral.

## 03 Research and contact plan

### Five minute preparation

- [ ] Confirm the business name, trade, service area, owner or decision contact, and public business contact details.
- [ ] Open the site on mobile. Check the main action, services, contact details, language options, and estimate form without submitting a fake inquiry.
- [ ] Write one factual observation and one question. Example: "I couldn't find an estimate form on the mobile homepage. How do customers usually request one?"
- [ ] Choose the website or AI opening. Have the relevant portfolio or working demo ready.
- [ ] Check prior contacts, language, last outcome, and any request to stop outreach.

### Suggested cold outreach sequence

Use one record per business across all channels. The following is a starting experiment, not a required volume or conversion benchmark.

- **Day 1:** one manual call. If unanswered, a short voicemail and one relevant email, if a suitable business address is available.
- **Day 3:** one personal DM to the business account if it is active. Skip it if already in a conversation elsewhere.
- **Day 6:** a second call at a different suitable business time. Reference the original topic.
- **Day 10:** one email with a useful observation or a permission-based demo offer.
- **Day 15:** a final email or DM on the channel used before. Close the sequence if there is no reply.

Stop the sequence when the person responds, books, declines, or requests no further contact. Use the follow-up plan on page 19 for an active opportunity. Avoid repeating every touch across every channel.

### Practical contact rules

Use manual calls and personal messages for this playbook. Identify Azul, use accurate subjects, and respect channel rules and contact preferences. Use a maintained business mailing address and an easy opt-out in commercial emails. Keep a shared do-not-contact list. Do not treat a public phone number as permission for AI calls, automated texts, or a prospect-specific agent deployment.

### Opening approach

Lead with the business and the observation. Explain AI only after confirming a task it could help with. Do not claim that the owner is losing money, that the site is broken, or that Azul already works with a competitor unless verified.

## 05 AI agent cold call

**Goal:** discover a repeatable problem. Start with the owner's process. The $300/month starting tier is not a universal voice or integration package.

### English

**Open:** "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Azul in South Florida. We build bilingual AI assistants for specific business tasks. Quick question: when someone contacts [Business] while the team is busy, what happens next?"

**Explore:** "Who responds?" "How long does that usually take?" "Is that something you'd like to improve, or is it handled well already?"

**If the problem is real:** "An assistant may be able to handle [confirmed task] and send exceptions to your team. We'd need to check your tools and test the workflow first. Would a 20-minute call to map that out be useful?"

**If they ask price:** "Custom agents start at $2,000 setup and $300 a month. The channel, integrations, and usage affect the quote. I'd want to understand the task before telling you which price applies."

### Español

**Inicio:** "Hola [Nombre], soy [Rep], de Azul, aquí en el sur de Florida. Creamos asistentes de AI bilingües para tareas específicas del negocio. Una pregunta rápida: cuando alguien contacta a [Negocio] y el equipo está ocupado, ¿qué pasa después?"

**Explorar:** "¿Quién responde?" "¿Cuánto tiempo suele pasar?" "¿Le gustaría mejorar esa parte, o ya la tienen bien resuelta?"

**Si hay un problema:** "Un asistente podría encargarse de [tarea confirmada] y pasar los casos especiales a su equipo. Primero revisaríamos sus herramientas y probaríamos el proceso. ¿Le serviría una llamada de 20 minutos para verlo juntos?"

**Precio:** "Los agentes a la medida empiezan en $2,000 de configuración y $300 al mes. El canal, las integraciones y el uso cambian la cotización. Primero necesito entender la tarea para decirle qué precio corresponde."

### Route the response

- **Already has staff:** ask which repetitive task the staff would like taken off their plate. Do not promise to replace a person.
- **Few inquiries:** explore administrative work only if the owner names a worthwhile problem. Low volume may make an agent a poor investment.
- **Wants phone callbacks:** distinguish this from web chat or text. AI Lead Agent is described in the current source as an HVAC pilot, with roofing next. Confirm current delivery availability with the founder.
- **No interest:** "Understood. I'll close this out. Thank you." / "Entendido. Cierro el seguimiento. Gracias."

## 09 Run the discovery call

**Suggested length:** 20-25 minutes. Spend the first half learning, then show only relevant work. Offer English or Spanish. Take notes in the buyer's words.

### Set the agenda

**EN:** "Let's understand how you handle [topic], look at one relevant example, and see whether a project makes sense. If it does, we'll agree on the scope and next step. What would make this call useful for you?"

**ES:** "Primero vemos cómo maneja [tema], después le muestro un ejemplo relevante y revisamos si tiene sentido hacer un proyecto. Si encaja, acordamos el alcance y el siguiente paso. ¿Qué le gustaría resolver en esta llamada?"

### Ask these questions in order

1. **EN:** "Where do new customers come from today, and what happens after they contact you?" **ES:** "¿De dónde llegan sus clientes nuevos y qué pasa después de que lo contactan?"
2. **EN:** "Which part causes the most trouble? Can you walk me through the last time it happened?" **ES:** "¿Qué parte le causa más problemas? ¿Me cuenta la última vez que pasó?"
3. **EN:** "How often does it happen? What does it cost you in time, rework, or missed opportunities?" **ES:** "¿Con qué frecuencia pasa? ¿Qué le cuesta en tiempo, trabajo repetido u oportunidades?"
4. **EN:** "What have you tried, and what would you want done differently?" **ES:** "¿Qué ha intentado y qué le gustaría que fuera diferente?"
5. **EN:** "If we improve this, what would you want to see or measure?" **ES:** "Si mejoramos esto, ¿qué resultado le gustaría ver o medir?"
6. **EN:** "What timing and investment would be workable? Who else needs to review the decision?" **ES:** "¿Qué fecha y presupuesto serían viables? ¿Quién más necesita revisar la decisión?"

### Confirm before demonstrating

**EN:** "What I'm hearing is [problem], which affects [consequence]. You want [outcome] by [timing]. Is that accurate?"

**ES:** "Por lo que me cuenta, [problema] afecta [consecuencia]. Quiere lograr [resultado] para [fecha]. ¿Lo entendí bien?"

Proceed when there is a concrete problem, an owner for the decision, a plausible budget, and a feasible next step. If a fact is unknown, write it down and assign a follow-up. A polite conversation alone is not a qualified deal.

## 10 Website discovery and demonstration

### Confirm the website scope

Ask which services and areas need pages; what action visitors should take; who supplies logos, photos, and content; whether English and Spanish are needed; who controls the domain; and whether the buyer expects forms, online payment, a store, logins, or integrations.

The current standard offer describes 8-15 pages. Confirm the exact page list and what support covers. E-commerce, client portals, logins, integrations, and other custom functions need a separate quote. SEO setup is not an ongoing ranking or lead-generation commitment.

### Five minute demonstration

1. Open the prospect's actual site on mobile. Ask permission to discuss the observed issue. Show it accurately without exaggerating its effect.
2. Open a relevant example from Azul's current work page. For roofing, Aspire is the closest listed example. Explain the visible work you can verify.
3. Follow the visitor's path: service information, trust information, and the action to request an estimate. Do not submit live test leads to a client business.
4. Switch languages if applicable. Explain what the proposed scope would include.
5. Ask the buyer to assess the change. Then confirm scope and price.

**EN:** "You mentioned customers need a clearer way to [action]. Here is how that path is organized in this example. For your site, we'd confirm [pages and features]. Which part would help most, and what is missing?"

## 12 AI demonstration

### Prepare a working example

Choose the task the buyer confirmed. Use clearly fictional names and requests. Before the meeting, test both languages, the connection to the destination tool, and the human handoff. If the demo is a prototype or a recording, say so. A transcript or animation is not proof that a live workflow works.

### Six minute demonstration

1. **Recap:** state the current process and the one part the agent would handle.
2. **Normal case:** show an inquiry, the required questions, and the real output. For booking, confirm an appointment exists in the test calendar rather than trusting the chat message alone.
3. **Spanish case:** repeat a short customer request in Spanish and verify the output.
4. **Unknown case:** ask something outside approved answers. Show how it avoids inventing an answer.
5. **Handoff case:** request a person or simulate a tool problem. Show where the owner receives the case.
6. **Review:** ask whether this matches the buyer's workflow and what must change.

### Introduce the demo

**EN:** "You said [task] takes [time or effort]. This example handles [bounded task]. I'm using sample data. The parts we would still need to confirm for your business are [tools, access, usage, and exceptions]."

**ES:** "Me comentó que [tarea] le toma [tiempo o esfuerzo]. Este ejemplo se encarga de [tarea delimitada]. Estoy usando datos de ejemplo. Para su negocio todavía necesitamos confirmar [herramientas, accesos, uso y excepciones]."

### Ask for an assessment

**EN:** "Would that output be useful to your team? What would stop you from trusting it with this task?"

**ES:** "¿Ese resultado le serviría a su equipo? ¿Qué tendría que resolver para confiarle esta tarea?"

### When it fails

**EN:** "That step didn't work as intended. I'll record it, confirm the cause with our builder, and show you the corrected result before you decide."

**ES:** "Ese paso no funcionó como debía. Lo voy a documentar, revisar con quien lo construye y mostrarle el resultado corregido antes de que decida."

Do not improvise a claim that the production system already solves a demo failure. Make the next meeting about the specific open requirement.

## 13 Explain value and the full cost

Use the buyer's numbers. Label unknowns as assumptions. Revenue, gross profit, and cash saved are different measures. A website does not create traffic by itself, and an agent does not create new demand merely by answering inquiries.

### Website example

At the confirmed standard rate, the build plus twelve monthly payments is **$2,100**: $1,500 + (12 x $50). This excludes separately quoted work and any other charges stated in the agreement.

If the buyer earns **$700 in contribution per additional job**, it takes three genuinely incremental jobs to cover $2,100. This is arithmetic with a hypothetical margin, not an expected result. Ask the buyer for a realistic figure after job costs. Never use the full job price as profit.

### AI example

At the lowest stated starting rates, setup plus twelve monthly payments is **$5,600**: $2,000 + (12 x $300). The actual quote may be higher. Included usage, extra usage, third-party costs, and scope changes must be explicit.

For a purely illustrative task, 20 hours per month valued at $25/hour equals $500 in monthly capacity. Against a $300 base fee, the difference is $200 before other costs. A $2,000 setup would take ten months to recover at that difference. This assumes the hours are actually recovered and valuable. Time released is not automatically a payroll saving.

### Talk through the assumptions

**EN:** "Let's use your numbers rather than guess. How much time would this realistically free up, or what is the contribution from an extra job? Which assumptions should we test before making a decision?"

**ES:** "Usemos sus números. ¿Cuánto tiempo podría liberar de verdad, o cuánto le queda de un trabajo adicional después de los costos? ¿Qué supuestos debemos comprobar antes de decidir?"

### Show the price without hiding recurring fees

Separate the build or setup fee, monthly fee, included scope, usage allowance, extra usage, and other charges. Explain when each payment begins only after the billing schedule is approved. Calculate comparisons using the actual quoted amount, not the lowest advertised starting point.

If the economics do not make sense, narrow the task, choose the website first, revisit later, or recommend no project. Do not force a return calculation to justify the offer.

## 20 Manage the pipeline

Use one customer relationship management system or one shared lead log. A tool is optional; accurate notes and dated actions are essential. The same rep owns the record through delivery handoff.

### Stages and evidence required

- **Researched:** business/contact verified, one observation recorded, initial offer chosen.
- **Contacted:** channel, date, message or call outcome recorded. No meaningful reply yet.
- **Connected:** buyer responded and a real problem is being explored.
- **Qualified:** problem, desired outcome, decision-maker, budget discussion, timing, and feasibility recorded. Discovery supports a project.
- **Demo completed:** relevant work demonstrated, buyer reaction and open requirements captured.
- **Proposal sent:** approved scope, price, recurring fees, dependencies, and review date delivered.
- **Decision pending:** buyer is reviewing a specific remaining issue by an agreed date.
- **Closed won:** signed agreement and agreed initial payment complete; onboarding owner and date assigned. Track verbal yes, signed, and payment pending separately until then.
- **Closed lost:** buyer declined or chose another option; reason captured. **Nurture:** a specific future trigger and suitable contact date recorded.

### Required fields

Business; trade; city; website; decision-maker; contact details and source; preferred language; contact preferences; owner; offer; current process; observed or confirmed problem; volume or impact; desired outcome; budget; other stakeholders; timing; stage; last contact; next action; due date; scoped fee; monthly fee; usage terms; open requirements; proposal status; agreement status; payment status; outcome reason.

**ES:** "Para confirmar: yo haré [acción] para [fecha], usted [acción] y volvemos a hablar el [fecha/hora]. ¿Correcto?"

## 22 First two weeks and coaching

### Prepare before outreach

The founder confirms commercial terms on page 23. The rep practices the website and AI openings in both languages, tests the demos, sets up a lead log, and selects approved portfolio links. Start with 20 carefully researched roofing and HVAC businesses.

### Suggested daily routine

For a full day, try 30 minutes for replies and follow-up; 45 minutes researching five new businesses; two 45-minute calling blocks; 30 minutes for personal emails and DMs; and remaining time for booked calls, proposals, and notes. For a shorter day, reduce new outreach before dropping commitments to active buyers.

Treat five researched businesses, ten manual call attempts, and five personalized written messages as **initial capacity targets to test**, not industry benchmarks or expected results. Count messages across channels; avoid sending every type to every company. Adjust after five working days using actual time and reply quality.

### Track a few useful measures

- Calls attempted and conversations with the appropriate decision-maker. Conversation rate = decision-maker conversations / call attempts.
- Written messages delivered and meaningful replies. Reply rate = meaningful replies / delivered written messages. Exclude bounces and automated replies.
- Meetings booked and held. Attendance = held meetings / meetings scheduled to occur in the period.
- Qualified opportunities, proposals, and outcomes. Compare wins with proposals from the same cohort after enough time for decisions; do not divide this week's wins by unrelated new proposals.
- Signed value, initial cash collected, and monthly fees contracted. Keep these separate.
- Loss reasons, frequent objections, and overdue next actions.

### Practice each day

Run a ten-minute role-play: skeptical roofer, busy HVAC owner, existing website, uncertain AI buyer, or partner approval. Score 0-2 for accurate opening, useful discovery, correct offer, truthful evidence, clear fees, and dated next step. Total possible: 12. This is a coaching tool, not a performance quota.

### Review after two weeks

Which trade engaged? Which observed problem led to a useful call? Where did deals stop? Revise one opening or question at a time. Keep actual phrasing that earns clear answers. Expand to other industries only with relevant examples and a delivery process the founder can support.

## 23 Commercial settings and source notes
