# Azul go-to-market strategy

### Focus for the next 90 days

Win a small group of South Florida HVAC clients through founder-led sales, prove the enquiry-to-estimate workflow, and turn that evidence into repeatable sales. Website Building and Speed-to-Lead Agent are the two offers. Lead with the problem the owner confirms.

**Recommended starting assumptions.** Evelio handles sales and coordinates delivery. Begin in Miami-Dade, then add nearby Broward accounts where introductions are available. Focus the first six weeks on HVAC. Add roofing after the first repeatable launches. These choices and all volume targets below are planning hypotheses.

**Positioning.** Azul helps South Florida home-service businesses turn website enquiries into conversations and booked estimates through a bilingual website and a managed Speed-to-Lead Agent.

**Buyer outcome.** A homeowner can request service in English or Spanish. During configured calling hours, the agent calls, asks approved questions, checks calendar availability and passes exceptions to the team. The owner sees the lead and next step in the workspace.

### Ninety-day objective

Aim for 3-5 paying clients, with at least two agent launches working through the complete intake, call, booking and handoff flow. Capture permission to use one factual case study. Treat these as execution targets. Reforecast after the first month using actual conversations, sales time and delivery capacity.

The package produces $450 monthly recurring revenue per client at the current internal rate. Three to five package clients would add $1,350-$2,250 MRR and $13,500-$22,500 in contracted setup fees before costs, payment timing and cancellations. Website-only sales have a $2,000 setup reference and contribute no assumed recurring revenue to this model.

### Why bilingual delivery belongs in the offer

Census QuickFacts reports that 75.3% of Miami-Dade residents age five and older spoke a language other than English at home in 2020-2024. This supports testing bilingual positioning locally. Ask each business which languages its customers use and demonstrate those paths.

# Pick accounts with a reason to act

### Primary customer profile

Start with an owner-led HVAC company serving Miami-Dade that receives website enquiries, can take additional estimate appointments and has an owner or dispatcher responsible for callbacks. A starting list can emphasize teams of roughly 2-15 field staff. Confirm size in conversation.

**Agent fit.** The owner describes delays reaching inbound form leads, an office team that cannot always respond, or an inconsistent handoff from form to calendar. They can share a supported Google Calendar, define service boundaries and assign a person to exceptions.

**Website fit.** Their mobile website has a specific observed issue in the route to an enquiry, lacks the language experience their customers request, or needs replacing. The owner can provide content, approve work and authorize domain changes.

**Package fit.** Both needs are confirmed and the business can support a combined launch. If their website already works, connect the agent to the existing form.

### Build the first list

Research 100 businesses across Miami, Hialeah, Doral, Kendall and nearby service areas. Use public business listings, company websites, local introductions and existing contacts. Record the business, trade, city, website, contact source, owner or office contact, language preference, one verified observation and outreach history.

Score each account one point for each confirmed condition: reachable decision-maker, website enquiry source, response problem, appointment capacity and willingness to provide calendar access. Research unknowns during discovery. Prioritize confirmed 4-5 point accounts for agent proposals.

### Qualify out early

An owner seeking additional traffic may need a different acquisition service before an agent is valuable. A business at full capacity may have no reason to accelerate bookings. A business seeking general inbound reception, social lead-ad integration or a custom CRM workflow needs separate feasibility review. Record the gap and a revisit date when relevant.

### Competitor discovery

For the first ten qualified conversations, ask what they use today, monthly spend, who owns it and why they might change. Include their receptionist, CRM automation, current web provider and manual callbacks in the comparison. Compare the buyer's actual workflow on language, calendar access, escalation, response timing, management effort and total cost.

Quote the two current services and verify any additional feature before including it.

### Sell a specific starting scope

Website scope specifies pages, languages, content responsibilities, forms, domain work, revision rounds, hosting and support. Agent scope specifies the connected form, business number, language paths, Google Calendar, calling hours, retry settings, transfer recipient and exception handling. Give a launch estimate after checking those inputs and current delivery capacity.

Offer a defined test and acceptance process. Show a form submission, test call, appointment and owner notification using numbers and inboxes you control. Record who accepts the result and what would delay launch.

### Budget as a controlled experiment

Proposed first-month cash allowance is up to $300 for outreach tools, demo/provider usage and local prospect meetings. Founder time is tracked separately. Approve each spend before purchase. Start with direct outreach, warm introductions and proof content.

Keep paid acquisition at $0 during initial validation. After two successful launches and a measured qualified-to-paid conversion rate, consider a separately approved $300 channel test. Choose one audience and one offer, and stop when the budget is reached.

### Unit economics review

For each client track setup revenue, build and onboarding hours, contractor costs, monthly subscription, Vapi/Twilio usage, email, hosting and monthly support hours. Monthly contribution equals subscription minus direct delivery and support cost. Track blended acquisition cost as cash acquisition spend plus valued sales time divided by new paying clients.

At $450/month with an illustrative $120 monthly direct cost, contribution is $330 before overhead and taxes. A $660 acquisition cost would recover in two months of contribution, excluding setup margin. Replace those assumptions with real costs before expanding spend.

# Acquire conversations and evidence

### Channel one - founder outreach

For the first four weeks, research 25 new accounts each week and make 50 total personalized touches across new outreach and follow-up. Use manual business calls, relevant emails and active business social profiles. Start with one verified observation or a question about their inbound lead process.

Offer a 15-minute lead-response walkthrough. Review the mobile enquiry path together, ask who responds and demonstrate the relevant workflow if there is a fit. Capture the buyer's words for the problem and the next action.

### Channel two - warm introductions

Ask satisfied contacts and local web, marketing or trade partners for an introduction to one HVAC owner with a confirmed response problem. Make five relevant partner approaches each week. Explain how Azul can connect a lead form to calling and calendar booking.

Agree ownership of introductions and communication before sharing client information. Any referral payment or commercial partnership needs its own agreed terms.

### Channel three - proof content

Publish two short pieces weekly using a controlled demo or approved client evidence. Show an English or Spanish enquiry becoming a call, a calendar event or an owner handoff. Label demonstrations clearly. End with an invitation to review the prospect's enquiry process.

For a case study, record dates, eligible leads, test exclusions, calling hours, attempts, bookings and how the owner verified results. Show a measured baseline where available. Obtain client approval before publishing screenshots, recordings or identifiable results.

### Validation tests

| Test | Sample and measure | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Response-delay opening versus bilingual opening | Alternate across 20 comparable accounts each. Count qualified meetings per contacted account. | Repeat the stronger opening on another sample before standardizing. |
| Website need versus agent need | Tag the first 10 completed discoveries by confirmed problem. | Allocate the next outreach list toward demonstrated fit. |
| Referral channel | Track 20 partner approaches over four weeks and resulting qualified introductions. | Keep partners who produce relevant conversations. Revise the request if none do. |

Keep language preference and account quality visible when comparing results. Small samples guide the next experiment.

# Execute in three phases

### Days 1-14 - establish the sales motion

**Evelio owns** the list, outreach and qualification. Select 50 accounts, prepare one English and one Spanish demo, and practice the full test-call flow on your own number. Make the first 100 touches, hold discovery calls and record reasons for interest or refusal.

Prepare a concise proposal template, a scope checklist and a shared pipeline before quoting. Check all public claims against current delivery, especially texts, integrations, hours and reporting. Review pricing privately with qualified buyers.

### Days 15-30 - close and launch the first cohort

Aim for two paid starts, subject to the observed funnel and delivery capacity. Give each signed client an onboarding owner, missing-input list and scheduled kickoff. Begin with a proposed capacity limit of one new agent launch per week until actual delivery hours are known.

Review launch failures before accepting overlapping deadlines. Follow the first genuine enquiries through the workspace, calendar and alert inbox. Ask the owner whether the workflow solves the discovery problem.

### Days 31-60 - tighten the offer

Review the first cohort's launch time, provider cost, support load and customer feedback. Complete one approved case study. Reuse objections and successful demos in outreach. Contact the next 100 researched accounts and develop the partners who produced useful introductions.

Add roofing only when two complete agent launches have passed acceptance and existing clients have reliable callback ownership. Keep the outreach message and qualification rules specific to that trade.

### Days 61-90 - expand the repeatable path

Aim for 3-5 total paying clients. Increase account volume only when calls lead to qualified opportunities and launches remain supportable. Evaluate the approved paid-channel experiment against contribution economics. Ask successful owners for one relevant introduction.

### Weekly operating rhythm

Monday reviews pipeline and delivery capacity. Tuesday through Thursday reserve daily outreach, discovery and follow-up blocks. Friday reviews funnel movement, cash, support effort and one sales experiment. Schedule prospect calls around the owner's availability.

# Measure and decide

### Working funnel for the first month

Plan for 100 unique researched accounts, 200 total touches, 20 two-way conversations, eight held discoveries, four qualified proposals and two paid starts. Repeat contacts count as touches. A conversation requires a reply or live exchange.

The implied rates are 20% account-to-conversation, 40% conversation-to-held-discovery, 50% discovery-to-proposal and 50% proposal-to-paid. These are planning assumptions for a small founder-led test, with no claim to industry benchmarks.

### Friday scorecard

| Measure | What to record | What a gap suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts contacted and conversations | Unique accounts, touches and responses by channel | Low reach suggests contact or list problems |
| Meetings held | Held meetings divided by booked meetings | Missed meetings suggest reminder or scheduling problems |
| Qualified proposals and paid starts | Counts, value, time in stage and loss reason | Weak closing suggests fit, proof, terms or decision access |
| Delivery | Days from complete inputs to acceptance and hours used | Delays suggest a scope or capacity issue |
| Client value | Eligible leads, call outcomes, verified visits and owner feedback | Activity without useful outcomes needs workflow review |
| Economics | Cash collected, MRR, direct costs and support time | Thin contribution requires scope or price review |

### Decisions when results stall

After 50 accounts with fewer than five conversations, check contact quality and opening relevance before increasing volume. After ten held discoveries with fewer than three qualified opportunities, narrow the audience or change the problem hypothesis. After five proposals with no paid start, review recordings or notes for decision access, proof, economics and unresolved risk.

When launches or support exceed available time, move proposed dates and improve setup before expanding acquisition. Review the strategy at days 30, 60 and 90 using the same definitions.

### First Monday checklist

Choose the first 25 accounts. Verify the demo number and calendar. Block outreach time. Prepare the pipeline fields. Contact five warm prospects. End each conversation with a specific next action and date.
